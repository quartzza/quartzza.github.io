<header class="wrap hero" id="story"><div><div class="eyebrow">Tackling the Conventional Wisdom</div><h1>Do critics predict<br>box office success?</h1><p class="lead">We test whether higher critic <b>Metascores</b> are associated with higher worldwide box office revenue — while accounting for budget, release year, and genre.</p><p><span class="tag">385 films</span><span class="tag">2022–2025*</span><span class="tag">HC3 robust SE</span><span class="tag">Observational data</span></p></div><div class="hero-card"><div class="small">KEY RESULT</div><div class="big">+22.9%</div><div>modeled worldwide gross for a 10-point Metascore difference</div><div class="small" style="margin-top:10px">Multiple regression · p = 0.00077 · 95% CI [9.0%, 38.5%]</div></div></header>
<main>
<section><div class="wrap"><h2>01 · The question</h2><p>Our main hypothesis is that a higher critic Metascore is associated with higher worldwide box office revenue. A second hypothesis asks whether that relationship differs across Action, Comedy, Drama, and Horror.</p><div class="grid"><div class="card"><div class="small">H₀</div><h3>β₁ = 0</h3><p>Metascore has no linear association with log worldwide revenue, conditional on the model.</p></div><div class="card"><div class="small">H₁</div><h3>β₁ ≠ 0</h3><p>Metascore is associated with log worldwide revenue.</p></div><div class="card"><div class="small">Moderation</div><h3>Genre × Metascore</h3><p>Metascore slopes may differ by genre.</p></div></div></div></section>
<section id="data"><div class="wrap"><h2>02 · What data did we use?</h2><p>The analysis starts from 407 USD-converted movie records. After validation and sample restrictions, 385 movies with one of the four target genres enter the modeling sample.</p><div class="grid"><div class="card"><div class="small">INPUT</div><div class="metric">407</div><p>movies in the embedded dataset</p></div><div class="card"><div class="small">MODELING SAMPLE</div><div class="metric">385</div><p>movies after validation + target-genre assignment</p></div><div class="card"><div class="small">MULTI-LABEL</div><div class="metric">143</div><p>movies contain more than one target genre</p></div></div><div class="callout warning"><b>Important:</b> 2025 is only partially covered. The sample is observational and complete-case restrictions may reduce representativeness.</div><div id="scatter" class="plot"></div></div></section>
<section id="stats"><div class="wrap"><h2>03 · Statistical test</h2><p>We compare a simple model with a multiple regression. The multiple model controls for log budget, release year, and assigned primary genre. Standard errors use HC3 robust estimation.</p><div class="grid2"><div class="card"><h3>Model comparison</h3><table class="table"><thead><tr><th>Model</th><th>R²</th><th>β Metascore</th><th>p</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Simple</td><td>0.004</td><td>−0.0095</td><td>0.2048</td></tr><tr><td>Multiple</td><td>0.582</td><td>+0.0206</td><td>0.00077</td></tr></tbody></table><p class="small">The large change shows why confounding controls matter for this dataset.</p></div><div class="card"><h3>Genre interaction</h3><div class="metric">p = 0.0493</div><p>Joint Wald test for whether Metascore slopes differ across the four genres.</p><table class="table"><tr><th>Genre</th><th>10-point association</th><th>p</th></tr><tr><td>Action</td><td>+45.8%</td><td>0.00001</td></tr><tr><td>Comedy</td><td>−0.7%</td><td>0.9489</td></tr><tr><td>Drama</td><td>+16.8%</td><td>0.1493</td></tr><tr><td>Horror</td><td>+21.5%</td><td>0.4096</td></tr></table></div></div><div id="ci" class="plot"></div></div></section>

<section id="myth"><div class="wrap"><h2>05 · Myth vs. Reality</h2><div class="grid2"><div class="card mythbox"><div class="small">CONVENTIONAL WISDOM</div><div class="myth">“A higher critic score means a movie will make more money.”</div></div><div class="card mythbox"><div class="small">WHAT OUR DATA SHOW</div><div class="reality">Higher Metascore is positively associated with modeled worldwide gross after controlling for budget, year, and genre. <section id="system"><div class="wrap"><h2>06 · System map</h2><p>Our analysis separates statistical evidence from theoretical factors that may also shape box office outcomes.</p><div class="map"><div class="node"><b>Critic Metascore</b><br><span class="small">Observed predictor</span></div><div class="arrow">→</div><div class="node"><b>Worldwide Gross</b><br><span class="small">Observed outcome</span></div><div class="node"><b>Budget</b><br><span class="small">Strong predictive factor</span></div><div class="arrow">↘</div><div class="node"><b>Marketing / Screens / Stars / IP / Timing</b><br><span class="small">Theory: possible hidden or unmeasured features</span></div></div><div class="callout warning"><b>Do not read arrows as causal proof.</b> Feature importance and regression coefficients describe statistical relationships under the model; they do not establish causal effects.</div></div></section>
<section id="limits"><div class="wrap"><h2>07 · Limits & robustness</h2><div class="grid"><div class="card"><h3>Observational</h3><p>No randomized treatment. Reverse causation and unmeasured confounding remain possible.</p></div><div class="card"><h3>Genre coding</h3><p>About 143/385 films have multiple target genres. The moderation result is near p=.05 and is sensitive to coding choices.</p></div><div class="card"><h3>2025 coverage</h3><p>2025 is partial. The 2022–2024 sensitivity check gives 22.2% vs 22.9% in the main analysis.</p></div></div><div class="callout"><b>Bottom line:</b> The strongest evidence is a positive adjusted association between Metascore and modeled worldwide gross. The result is useful for decision-making, but should not be presented as a causal effect.</div></div></section>
